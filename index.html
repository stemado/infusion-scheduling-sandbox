Assumptions that make the comparison fair

  • One chair and one nurse are available 8:00–12:00. No other bookings use them.
  • All three visits are suitable for this chair and nurse. Each keeps its full duration.
  • Starts are sequential. No breaks, turnover or other care demands are modeled.
  • No waiting 240-minute visit has priority for this particular opening. Actual demand must be checked.

A scheduling hypothesis to investigate

    About this fictional model

    All people, appointments, schedules, queues, staffing levels and rules in this demonstration are fictional. Nurse A, Nurse B and Nurse C are role labels. This is a general educational example, not a description or evaluation of any hospital, person or software product.

    “Illustrated behavior” shows the starting restrictions. “Desired behavior” explores alternatives under the stated assumptions. Results are proposals, not confirmed appointments, operational recommendations or evidence about a real system.

    The long-block example uses a hypothetical exact-duration rule. It assumes a suitable chair and nurse are available throughout the block, no waiting long visit has priority for that opening, and an appropriate release condition can be met. Releasing time for shorter visits removes the continuous four-hour opportunity. Queue and usage counters describe this small fictional example only.

    Each patient stays with one nurse throughout the visit. Capacity releases at the end. Breaks, turnover, treatment complexity, staff skills, other bookings and patient preferences are not modeled. The one-start-at-a-time rule is an illustrative assumption, not a clinical recommendation.

    The assignment examples keep existing bookings fixed except for the explicit room reassignment. A later appointment may resolve one conflict without increasing total throughput. Any real implementation would need its own requirements and validation.

    This self-contained page runs in the browser. It has no patient-data input, analytics, external scripts, network requests or clinical-system connections. Copy buttons write the displayed fictional discussion question to the clipboard.
